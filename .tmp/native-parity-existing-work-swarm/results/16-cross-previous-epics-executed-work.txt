## 5. Recommended plan adjustments

1. **Do not treat the conformance ledger as final.** `conformance.yaml` marks all 31 rows `implemented`, but the `Pipeline.native_program` gap and `components.py` survival contradict the North Star's "Done Means." Any new plan must re-verify the top ~5 load-bearing rows (prep-clarification-gate, gate decision, tiebreaker, execute-approval, override-surface) against runtime behavior, not just evidence-bundle hashes.

2. **Reuse, don't rebuild:** The `run_state/` resolver, `boundary_contracts.py` registry, `generate_native_representation_evidence.py` checker, and the AST purity scanner in `arnold.workflow.handler_semantics` are all solid, importable code. Build on them.

3. **Audit `components.py` extraction first.** The oracle synthesis (`docs/arnold/megaplan-native-oracle-synthesis.md:25-28`) identified `build_pipeline()` as *"the immediate high-risk function"* — it consumes `.pypeline` but rebuilds from `components.py`. This is still true. A new plan should make `build_pipeline()` consume lowered `.pypeline` topology exclusively as milestone zero.

4. **Wire the run-state resolver into consumers.** The `canonical-run-state-control-plane` NORTHSTAR lists 11 integration points (watchdog, Discord, repair-loop, etc.). The `_northstar.py` smoke test proves the resolver works. Wiring it is lower risk than new greenfield.

5. **Treat `workflow.pypeline` as an authored skeleton, not a proven semantic owner.** Its 754 lines are mostly `DECLARED_STEP_INTERFACES` route tables (lines 81–300+) and override dispatch trees (lines 650–754) that still call through `AUTHORING_*` component constants. The report at `megaplan-native-representation-report.md:68-81` itself documents that the pypeline "does not fully own" prep internals, gate semantics, tiebreaker internals, execute dependency scheduling, or review mode selection.
