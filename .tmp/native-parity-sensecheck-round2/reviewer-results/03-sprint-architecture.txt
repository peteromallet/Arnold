# Lens 3 — Sprint Architecture, Granularity, and Dependency Audit

## 1. Verdict

**FAIL — conditional on amendment.** The s1–s7 compression is architecturally defensible in direction but unsafe in its current form. Three sprints (S1, S3, S5) over-bundle work that the 15-sprint swarm synthesis explicitly warned against merging. One sprint (S3) contains a deletion-before-migration hazard for front-half carriers. The dependency ordering from S2→S3→S4→S5 is fundamentally sound, and the semantic/custody dual-gate pattern is well-designed, but the compression loses three independent proof boundaries that the 15-sprint plan considered essential. The m1–m10 historical decomposition reveals that gate/revise coupling and execute fanout/review integration risks were correctly identified but then re-bundled in the compressed plan. **With the ranked amendments below, the plan becomes sound.**

## 5. Justified Compression and Sound Sequencing

### What the compression gets right

1. **S2 generic-primitives-first.** Building product-neutral constructs BEFORE migrating product phases is the correct dependency order. The 15-sprint plan had this right; the 7-sprint plan preserves it. S3-S6 all consume S2's output.

2. **S4 tiebreaker+finalize as coherent unit.** These share the human-decision machinery and durable reentry infrastructure. Bundling them avoids splitting the custody lifecycle across sprints.

3. **S6 auto/projection as consumers-only.** Placing override and auto-drive collapse AFTER all product phases are migrated ensures auto cannot silently own behavior during migration. Correct sequencing.

4. **S7 as aggregation, not implementation.** The brief explicitly says "extend—not duplicate—the admitted Custody M11 conformance framework" (S7:5). If S1-S6 tooling is built forward (as the swarm recommends), S7 is realistic aggregation.

5. **Dual semantic/custody gates.** Every sprint has two independent closure gates. This is the strongest architectural decision in the plan — it prevents the old pattern where a semantic-looking source passed because custody enforcement wasn't wired.

### What the compression loses

1. **Three independent proof boundaries.** The 15-sprint plan had S1a→S1b→S1c→S1d as infrastructure, S2a→S2b as front-half, S4a→S4b and S5a→S5b as delivery. Each split created a checkpoint where one domain's proof didn't depend on another's bugs. The 7-sprint plan collapses these, meaning a bug in S3's gate migration can block S3's critique proof from closing.

2. **build_pipeline() migration not explicit.** The `planning.py:695` function is the central coupling point — it reads from both lowered source and component metadata. S3 says it will "make build_pipeline() consume the lowered structure" but this is a ~200-line refactor of `_canonical_steps()`, `_canonical_routes()`, `_metadata_for_step()`, and `_policy_for_step()` — all of which currently read `PIPELINE_STEP_COMPONENTS_BY_ID` and `component.metadata`. Without explicit scope, S3 will either under-deliver or overrun.

3. **M11 prerequisite chain gap.** The `custody-control-plane` chain log shows only M1 activity. The corrective plan requires M11's "exact Run Authority, Custody, and WBC contract/schema versions; enabled enforcement cohort; zero-bypass controlled-writer inventory; exact-version WBC producer/query registries; transactional attempt/effect history; custody lease/epoch lifecycle; recovery, retry, cross-host transfer/reclaim" (`corrective-plan.md:48-56`). None of these are evident in the current chain state. The entire s1-s7 plan is conditional on this prerequisite — and it does not appear to exist yet.

### Net assessment

The 7-sprint plan is a defensible compression if and only if Amendments 1-6 are applied. The compression from 15 to 7 is not inherently reckless — much of the 15-sprint plan's splitting was cautious, and the swarm confirms significant existing substrate. But the three overloaded sprints (S1, S3, S5) each bundle 2-4 sprints of work, and the deletion-before-migration hazard in S3 is a hard safety issue. With the amendments, the plan becomes a sound 11-sprint execution (1a, 1b, 2, 3a, 3b, 4, 5a, 5b, 6, 7) with realistic load, independent proof boundaries, and correct deletion sequencing. Without them, S3 and S5 carry unacceptable risk of false closure or regression.

**The M11 prerequisite gap is the single largest risk to the entire epic.** If the custody control plane is not at M11 end state with a valid completion manifest, every sprint's custody-adoption gate is built against an API surface that does not exist. The chain's `launch_preconditions` would correctly block start — but the sprint briefs assume the surface is available and do not include contingency for its absence.
